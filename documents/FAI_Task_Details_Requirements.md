# Dataset & Resource Access Guide

*Student Challenge Resources | FarmwiseAI Product Challenge*

This guide explains the datasets, reference resources and access that FarmwiseAI will provide to shortlisted participants. FarmwiseAI will provide only the foundational resources described for each task. Participants are expected to independently source, verify, process and integrate additional data required to build their solution.

## Important — Read Before Starting

**The official FarmwiseAI Challenge Brief and the respective problem statement remain the primary reference for this challenge.** If you have any doubt about the objective, scope, expected output, technical requirements, constraints, evaluation criteria or interpretation of a task, please read the Challenge Brief and the relevant problem statement again before proceeding. This document is intended only to explain the resources being provided by FarmwiseAI; it does not replace the original brief.

**Task 1 has no separate dataset.** Participants must use relevant datasets and resources provided for Tasks 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 as inputs for Task 1. No additional Task 1 dataset package will be provided separately.

## Task 1 — Multi-Model, Multimodal & Agentic AI

- No separate dataset will be provided for Task 1.
- Use appropriate datasets/resources from Tasks 2, 3, 4, 5 and/or 6 as inputs to your Task 1 solution.
- The participant is responsible for selecting suitable data and demonstrating the multimodal, multi-model and agentic workflow, including any required processing, retrieval, integration and validation.

## Task 2 — Multimodal GeoAI & Land Document Intelligence

- FarmwiseAI will provide land documents for the designated parcel/area.
- FarmwiseAI will provide available cadastral/survey map data, including survey/parcel information.
- FMB (Field Measurement Book) maps or equivalent survey reference maps will be provided where available.
- Basic GIS reference layers will be provided for the study area, such as roads, water bodies, administrative boundaries and other relevant geographic layers available to FarmwiseAI.
- These resources are the starting reference set. Participants are responsible for OCR, extraction, document-to-parcel/survey matching, spatial analysis, discrepancy detection and the final application/dashboard.

## Task 3 — Crop Monitoring / Satellite Intelligence

- FarmwiseAI will provide ground-truth reference points for the designated study area.
- The ground-truth points are reference information, not a complete ready-made solution.
- Participants are expected to verify and study the ground-truth points and independently carry out the further data acquisition, satellite intelligence, preprocessing, feature/embedding generation or selection, modelling and validation required for their use case.
- The participant is responsible for deciding the appropriate approach and producing the final crop-monitoring/classification model and output.

## Task 4 — Mobile Crop, Stage & Disease Intelligence

- FarmwiseAI will provide the crop photographs/reference images specified in the challenge resource package.
- Participants may use Google Gemini or another permitted multimodal model during development, including for teacher/reference labelling where appropriate.
- The final submission must be a developed/trained model or deployable inference solution. It must NOT simply require sending every new image manually to Google Gemini and using Gemini's response as the final output.
- The developed solution should accept an image as input and independently produce the required prediction, with an appropriate confidence, fallback or verification mechanism where applicable.
- Participants are responsible for training, validation, model development and the final inference workflow.

## Task 5 — Street View Urban Intelligence

- FarmwiseAI will provide the designated study area where the Street View analysis is to be performed.
- Participants are responsible for carrying out the Street View analysis within this study area using approved/authorized access methods and permitted public data sources.
- The analysis should cover broad street-level intelligence, not only whether a building is commercial or residential.
- Relevant elements may include traffic signals, electric poles, streetlights, utility infrastructure, road features, buildings, signage, visible assets and other useful street-level details.
- Participants are responsible for selecting useful imagery/views, detection/OCR methods, geospatial association, data integration and presentation of the resulting intelligence.

## Task 6 — Collector's District Intelligence Platform

- FarmwiseAI will provide access to approximately two to three approved websites/systems, together with the necessary credentials or access details.
- These sources are starting points, not the only sources that may be used. Participants are expected to incorporate other publicly available and permitted sources where useful.
- The final output should be a flexible Collector-facing platform in which the Collector can decide what information is useful and how the dashboard is organized.
- The platform should support adding a new source by providing its link and, where legitimately permitted, its access credentials/details, so that the source can be integrated into the Collector's dashboard.
- The intended outcome is a reusable district-intelligence platform that can bring together multiple sources and present relevant information, incidents, trends, locations and priorities in a form useful for administrative decision-making.

## Data Usage & Responsibility

- Use only data, APIs, websites, credentials and imagery through permitted and authorized access methods.
- Participants are responsible for checking the quality, relevance and validity of any additional data they source.
- Do not expose, redistribute or publish FarmwiseAI-provided credentials or restricted datasets.
- The final solution should clearly distinguish between FarmwiseAI-provided resources and data independently sourced or generated by the participant.
- FarmwiseAI-provided resources are intended as the starting point; participants are expected to demonstrate independent research, verification, engineering, modelling and integration.
